THE HISTORY OF MINECRAFT

From a small prototype to a worldwide phenomenon

⛏️ 2009 — 2026 🌍

OUR JOURNEY THROUGH THE BLOCKS

Explore the most important moments in Minecraft history!

2009 — The Prototype

Markus "Notch" Persson, working at the small studio Mjängatale (later Mojang), released Cave Game in early 2009, quickly changed to Minecraft.

Minecraft was a rough sandbox: break blocks, place blocks, survive the night. He sold early versions, pre-release versions, cheaply and let players watch it grow, building a community before there was a finished product.

2010–2011 — Minecraft Survival Mode

Years later, a more thorough development of the game began, adding health, hunger mechanics, the crafting process, and infinite world generations.

Multiplayer servers appeared almost immediately, and with them the first wave of Minecraft players' culture.

Version 1.0 released in November 2011 at Minecraft's own convention: "MineCon," with an "ending."

"The End," the newest addition to the game. This update turned into Minecraft's most famous and unique feature.

2012–2013 — Platforms and the Console Boom

The Xbox 360 (2012) and PlayStation consoles reached the players to their own living-rooms, and their phones. Sales climbed into the tens of millions.

Mojang kept adding biomes, the Nether, villages, and redstone, Minecraft's electricity version.

2014 — Microsoft's Epic Entrance

Microsoft acquired Mojang for $2.5 billion; "Notch" and Mojang co-founders left the company.

The move worried some fans but gave the game corporate-scale infrastructure, cross-platform ambitions, and a long runway.

2015–2017 — Spin-offs, Updates and More

📖 Minecraft: Story Mode

"Minecraft: Story Mode," tried the crazy combination of narrative and Minecraft. The game was mainly about advancing through its "Minecrafty" story by choosing different options or dialogues.

This game became one of the most loved Minecraft spin-offs' games for its many stories and brand-new additions to Minecraft's lore.

🌐 Better Together Update

The big structural change was the "Better Together" update in 2017, which unified console and mobile versions and enabled cross-play across devices — a genuinely rare thing in the industry at the time.

2018–2020 — Education, Earth, Dungeons, and a Pandemic Bump

🎓 Minecraft: Education Edition

Minecraft: Education Edition became a real classroom tool. This Minecraft version has been helping people to learn Chemistry and Coding since its launch, it also has many instructional books and manuals in-game to support people's learning.

🌎 Minecraft Earth

Minecraft Earth, a VR Pokémon Go-like game, came and went. It's better if we don't talk about that.

⚔️ Minecraft Dungeons

Minecraft Dungeons, a well-liked dungeon crawler spin-off.

🌍 The Pandemic

And when COVID-19 lockdowns hit, the game's social function — servers, realms, school groups building together — drove a huge surge in players and cultural relevance.

2021–2023 — The Caves & Cliffs Era and the Mob Vote

⛰️ Caves & Cliffs

Big world-generation overhauls, added in the "Caves & Cliffs" update, the Deep Dark and Warden, archaeology; in "Trails & Tales."

🐾 The Mob Vote

An annual event called "The Mob Vote," became a ritual for the community, as every tradition, it had occasional controversies.

🛡️ Minecraft Legends

Minecraft Legends was a spin-off under the genre of strategy game that didn't land as well. We would rather not talk about that either.

2024–2026 — What's New With Minecraft Now?

🎬 A Minecraft Movie

Well, "A Minecraft Movie," brought the game to cinemas and to a new, younger audience.

🔨 The Future of Minecraft

The game itself continues on a roughly annual update cadence, plus a thriving ecosystem of marketplace content, mods, and creator servers.

💎 300 Million Copies Sold

Sales have long since passed the 300 million copies, making it one of the biggest video games in history.

The videogame's very lively community and occasional updates — we better get more of those — keeps the old spirit, what "Notch" would have ever wanted for Minecraft and more.
